## Section by section: facts

**10. Talamoa** (stage `talamoa`, model `brain`)
- p3: the nervous system is our main system for relating to the surroundings (endocrine too). All stimuli (sight, smell, touch, pain, temperature) come in through it. A sensory neuron takes the stimulus towards the brain; sometimes it synapses in the cord with another sensory neuron, otherwise its first synapse is in the encephalon. Before reaching the cortex there must be synapses; the most important one is in the THALAMUS.
- p4: thalamus = the main sensory structure of the whole encephalon (it has motor components too). If a sensation does not synapse in the thalamus we are not conscious of it. ALL sensations EXCEPT SMELL: smell goes straight from the nose to the cortex (the most primitive).
- Steps (sketch): 1 stimulus at the skin → sensory neuron to the cord (posterior root ganglion) ; 2 the way up (synapse in the cord OR first synapse in the encephalon); 3 the thalamus lights (relay, "sinapsi garrantzitsuena"); 4 on to the sensory cortex: only now conscious; 5 smell's way: nose → cortex directly, bypassing the thalamus (`usaimena`, KONTUZ-like warn step: the one exception). `ask`: "Zein sentsazio ez da talamotik pasatzen?" → usaimena.

**11. Kortexa + 5.1** (stage `kortexa`, model `brain`, run-on from `talamoa`)
- 11 p3: the cortex processes the information. Sensory cortex passes it to the motor cortex (motor neurons there). That neuron sends it by its axon down the cord's white matter to the right level; the cord's motor neuron carries out the answer, out through the anterior root to the muscles: the muscle moves. Processing → a motor order; the order must reach the hand and arm muscles; from the top down, after some synapses, the last motoneuron reaches the muscle. All in milliseconds (pulling the hand away from pain).
- 5.1 p1–p2: reflex movements are much faster than voluntary ones: their sensory information does not reach the cortex but the cord; processed there, the motor neuron leaves from there. Glossary line: sensory = ascending pathways, motor = descending; making a synapse = a relay ("erreleboa"); motor neuron = motoneuron.
- Steps: sensory cortex → motor cortex (arrow across the central sulcus); descending axon down the white matter; the anterior-horn motoneuron (`motoneurona`) at the arm's level (cervical: shorter axon than to the leg, ties to section 4); out the anterior root to the arm muscle, the hand moves; compare: reflex arc stays in the cord (short loop, faster). Show ascending (blue) vs descending (red) as two lanes. `ask` here or in `talamoa`, not both.

**12. Bentrikulu sistema** (stage `bentrikuluak`, model `ventricles`)
- p3: all the CNS comes from the neural tube. In the cord it stays a tube; cranially it transforms (widens, narrows): the vesicles. A tube is hollow: in the cord that space is the ependyma / ependymal canal; in the brain the cavities widen into the ventricles (inside the vesicles).
- p4: the cavities are not empty: CSF runs through them, so all must communicate; in general they are called ventricles.
- p5: telencephalon (inside each hemisphere, one per vesicle) → 2 LATERAL ventricles (large in the adult as the hemispheres grow). Notes: do NOT call them I and II (they lie side by side) → KONTUZ step.
- p6: diencephalon (at the thalamus) → III ventricle. Lateral ventricles ↔ III by the interventricular foramina (Monro).
- p7: mesencephalon: keeps the tube form and narrows: the (cerebral / mesencephalic) aqueduct. (The eponym "Silvio" is in the notes; the app uses "garun akueduktoa / mesentzefalo akueduktoa".)
- p8: rhombencephalon (at metencephalon + myelencephalon): the IV ventricle, which keeps the rhomb shape.
- p9: it has 3 openings: 2 lateral (Luschka) and 1 median (Magendie).
- Steps: 1 the embryonic tube's lumen in its vesicles (colours as the vesicles, `C19.pros/mes/rhomb`), the caudal part a narrow canal; 2 the hemispheres grow, the lateral ventricles balloon out of the telencephalon (growth as growth: the cast inflates and curls into the C shape); 3 III ventricle in the diencephalon + Monro foramina; 4 the aqueduct narrows; 5 IV ventricle (rhomb) + its 3 openings; 6 continuous with the ependymal canal down the cord. Warn step on "not I and II".

**13. Likido zefalorrakideoaren ekoizpena** (stage `likido-zefalorrakideoa`, model `ventricles`, run-on)
- p3: CSF production starts in the 5th week. It is distributed through the CNS's inner vesicles and canals (ependyma, ventricles) and outside the CNS as well; main function protection (cushioning) so the brain takes no hard knocks. Continuous production in a limited fixed space → continuous drainage / reabsorption too. Choroid plexuses = networks of arterioles and venules, in the roofs of the lateral, III and IV ventricles: they produce the CSF. In the IV ventricle's roof lies the cerebellum.
- p4: flow: lateral ventricles → interventricular foramen (Monro) → III ventricle → aqueduct → IV ventricle → out through 3 openings (2 lateral, 1 median) → out of the ventricular system.
- p5: part keeps going down inside the ependymal canal (IV ventricle communicates with it); the rest leaves by the 3 openings to surround the CNS; after surrounding it, it passes through special cranial structures into the venous system (reabsorbed). Outside the CNS it runs in the subarachnoid space (between pia and arachnoid).
- Steps: 1 choroid plexuses (red-purple tufts) in the three roofs, CSF begins (5th week); 2 flow particles (`stream` on Item3: GPU copies along a route, scrubbable) lateral → Monro → III → aqueduct → IV; 3 the split at the IV: down the ependymal canal / out by the 3 openings; 4 the subarachnoid space fills round brain and cord (meninges from 19-A's head model: dura, arachnoid, pia, `C19.csf`); 5 reabsorbed into the venous system at the top (dural sinus, "kraneoko egitura bereziak"); 6 recap: production = drainage, cushioning. `ask`: order of the flow.
